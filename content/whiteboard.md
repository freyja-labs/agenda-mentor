The whiteboard is the only input the faculty member writes themselves, and it outranks everything else. The draft treats it as the faculty member's own statement of the problem; anything the board leaves blank is labeled as a suggestion. A good board takes 75 minutes and three people.

Meeting remotely, or no whiteboard in the room? [Type the board](type/index.html): the same regions with text boxes, kept in the faculty member's browser and sent straight to the prompt builder. A typed board needs no photo and no transcription prompt.

## Who is in the room

- **The faculty member** holds the marker and writes everything on the board.
- **The facilitator** asks the questions below, keeps time, and writes nothing.
- **The challenger**, a colleague from an adjacent field, plays the reviewer in the last twenty minutes.

Run it with three or more people. A cohort can run several boards at once at tables of three or four, with faculty rotating into the challenger seat for each other.

## Run sheet

1. **Field, area, topic (10 minutes).** The faculty member writes the top line. The facilitator asks: what would the field call this? What would a program officer call it?
2. **Objective (10 minutes).** One sentence in a box: what the work extracts, protects, explains, improves, or makes possible. The facilitator asks: if this works, what can someone do that they cannot do today?
3. **Three challenges (30 minutes, ten each).** Number them. Under each, write the works that started it ("who started this?") and the people working on it now ("who else is looking?"). If nobody is, write that down; it is either the opening or a warning.
4. **Challenge the board (15 minutes).** The challenger asks three questions of each challenge: does a product already do this? Would a program officer fund it? Can you start it with what you have today?
5. **Close (10 minutes).** Circle the challenge to lead with. Photograph the board. Agree who runs the transcription prompt and when the faculty member will confirm it.

## Photographing the board

A typed board has nothing to photograph; skip to the next section.

- Shoot straight on, with the whole board in the frame.
- Turn off the lights that cause glare, or step left or right until the glare clears the writing.
- Take a second, closer photo of any dense region.
- Run [prompt 1](../prompts/#p01-whiteboard-transcription) the same day, while everyone remembers what the abbreviations meant.

## After the session

The transcription prompt returns the board's text, the readings it is unsure of, probable corrections (misspelled names are common), and up to five questions. The faculty member answers the questions and confirms the transcription before the draft runs. A misspelled author name on the board becomes a wrong citation in the draft if nobody catches it here.

A typed board has no uncertain readings, so it skips prompt 1. Read it back to the faculty member, check the spelling of every name and title on it, and send it to the prompt builder.
