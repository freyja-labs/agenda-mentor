A mentor's job is to hold the process and protect the faculty member's judgment. The faculty member decides what to be known for, whom to work with, and what to decline. The mentor makes sure those decisions get made, in order, on evidence, and that nothing circulates before it is true.

## What a mentor does

- Reads the draft and the red-team review side by side before the session, with the claims ledger open.
- Asks the decisions one at a time and waits for the answer. Batched decisions get answered quickly and badly.
- Holds the gates: no working session before a sponsor, no consortium before a joint project, no circulation before every claim is supported.
- Opens doors the faculty member cannot open alone: an introduction to a program officer, a field leader, or a potential sponsor.

## What a mentor does not do

- Write the agenda. The faculty member drafts with the prompts; the mentor reviews.
- Promise sponsorship or partnership on anyone's behalf.
- Name partners, collaborators, or sponsors in the faculty member's document without their permission.

## Pairing

Two mentors work better than one: a senior colleague in the field, who knows the reviewers and the competitors, and a colleague outside it, who checks that a dean or a program officer can follow every paragraph. A cohort of four to six faculty members can share the outside mentor and red-team each other's drafts at tables of three.

## The red-team conversation

The review will be blunt; the conversation should be specific. Open with what holds up, because the assets in that table are the agenda's foundation. Then take the reviewer's findings in the order they would stop a program officer: claims the record does not support first, then the gap, then the make-or-break engine, then scope. End by agreeing the order of the decisions, and schedule the decisions session within two weeks, while the review is fresh.

Draft agendas are coaching documents. They stay between the faculty member and the mentors until version 2 passes the [verification checklist](../verify/).

## Review links in Facultory

When the faculty member keeps the agenda in [Facultory]({{facultory_signup}}), they can send you a review link in place of a document. It opens the current version without an account. If they allow comments, you comment on each section, and they resolve each comment as they revise. They can withdraw the link at any time, and it never shows their intake answers.
